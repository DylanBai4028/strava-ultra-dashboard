Load & fitness metrics

ACR — Acute:Chronic Ratio
Acute (7-day) load ÷ chronic (28-day) load, using Strava Relative Effort as the load unit.
Reading it: 0.8–1.3 is this dashboard's safe band. Below 0.8 usually means rest or detraining (expected during a deload). Above 1.3 is a warning; above 1.5 is flagged critical.
CTL — Chronic Training Load ("Fitness")
A 42-day exponentially-weighted average of daily training load — moves slowly on purpose.
Reading it: higher = a bigger aerobic base. CTL sat at 85–95 at the start of your last four races — track today's number against that range heading into the next one.
ATL — Acute Training Load ("Fatigue")
The same idea as CTL, but over 7 days — reacts fast to what you've just done.
Reading it: spikes after a big block, drops during a rest week. High ATL isn't bad on its own — sustained high ATL without recovery is the pattern to watch.
TSB — Training Stress Balance ("Form")
CTL − ATL: the gap between long-term fitness and short-term fatigue.
Reading it: above +15 is fresh, with room to load more. Below −10 is carrying real fatigue. Three of your last four races started between −58 and −78 (heavy fatigue); UTK started at +9 (fresh).
Ramp rate
How much CTL has changed over the last 7 days.
Reading it: this dashboard calls +3/week or more "building", −3/week or more "declining", and anything between "holding steady".
Relative Effort
Strava's own per-activity effort score — roughly duration × intensity, adjusted for heart rate where available.
Reading it: it's the raw ingredient behind ACR, CTL, ATL and TSB — all four are really just Relative Effort, summed and smoothed differently.

Heart rate & effort

HR — Heart Rate
Beats per minute during activity.
Z1–Z5 — heart rate zones
Five intensity bands from your device's zone boundaries — Z1 recovery, Z2 easy aerobic, Z3 moderate, Z4 threshold, Z5 max.
Reading it: endurance training generally wants a Z2-heavy distribution, with Z4–Z5 kept to a small share of total time.
HR coverage
% of activities that recorded heart-rate data.
Reading it: below full coverage, HR-dependent charts (aerobic efficiency, zone distribution) only reflect the runs that happened to record it, not your whole training.
Aerobic efficiency
Speed ÷ average heart rate, on qualifying easy runs only (Z2 or below, ≥5km, not a race).
Reading it: a rising trend means you're running faster at the same heart rate — a core sign of aerobic fitness improving, independent of how hard a session felt.

Running & training terms

Cadence
Steps per minute (spm).
Reading it: 170–180spm is a commonly cited road-running target, but ultra/trail cadence runs naturally lower and more variable — climbs, technical terrain, and fatigue all reduce it. Trend matters more than hitting a number.
Consistency score
Run days per week, out of 7 — a day count, not a distance or intensity measure.
Reading it: also shown as a % on Year over Year (days run ÷ 7). You can be consistent at low volume or inconsistent at high volume — they're different things.
Back-to-back days
Pairs of consecutive running days in a week.
Reading it: not flagged just for being high (you run near-daily, so most weeks would trip a fixed count) — flagged only when it jumps 3+ pairs more than the week before.
Easy run / Long run
Two run classifications used elsewhere on this dashboard. Easy run: HR at or below your Z2 ceiling, not a race, ≥5km. Long run: ≥21.1km (half-marathon or more).
Reading it: only easy runs feed the aerobic efficiency chart; only long runs feed the per-km pacing chart.
Deload
A planned period of reduced volume, for recovery — not a sign of a problem.
Reading it: the Europe trip is treated as a deload throughout this dashboard, so a low ACR or falling CTL during it isn't flagged as a warning.
Taper
A planned volume reduction in the final weeks before a race, to shed fatigue while keeping fitness and sharpness.
Reading it: the opposite goal of a deload — a taper is about race readiness, a deload is about recovery.
Detraining
The loss of fitness (falling CTL) from an extended drop in training volume.
Reading it: expected after the Europe deload — the post-Europe rebuild phase exists specifically to reverse it.

Race & data terms

UTMB
Ultra-Trail du Mont-Blanc — originally one race in the French Alps, now a global circuit of affiliated ultramarathons.
The Kosciuszko UTMB Miler is one of these affiliated races, not the original Mont-Blanc event.
Fixed-time event
A race format where the clock is fixed and distance is the result — run for a set duration (e.g. 24 hours), whoever covers the most distance wins.
The Southern Sydney 24 Hour Ultra in your race history is this format.
GPS-measured vs. official
A watch's GPS distance/elevation and a race's official course-measured figures are separate sources that routinely disagree (GPS drift, tree cover, barometric error).
Reading it: Race History uses official race figures as the source of truth, not Strava's GPS numbers — that's why they can look different from other Strava-based totals on this dashboard.
Elevation gain
Total meters climbed (ascent only, not descent) over a run or week.
Shown as "m+" in race facts, e.g. "5,131 m+".
Wear threshold (shoes)
The distance after which a shoe's cushioning has typically broken down enough to raise injury risk — 800km on this dashboard.
A rule of thumb, not a hard cutoff — some shoes/runners get more or less life out of a pair.

Checkpoint supplies — the pattern, not every cell

  • Water, electrolyte, most snacks (chips, pretzels, banana, orange, watermelon, bakery, bread), sunscreen/Vaseline and hygiene products: at every checkpoint.
  • Gels are not guaranteed at every checkpoint (missing at Trout Hatchery, Charlotte Pass Lookout, Perisher Ski Tube, Village Green per 2025) — carry your own supply rather than planning around course gels.
  • Hot food/drinks (soup, noodles, tea/coffee) only start from Guthega Base Camp (~88km) onward — nothing hot in roughly the first 90km/first ~19h.
  • Eagles Nest is a Water Point, not a full aid station — fluids only, no drop bag, limited access (Thredbo chairlift only).
  • Hot meal + finisher gift specifically for Miler/100 runners in the Recovery Area at the finish.
  • This is a cup-less event — bring your own (two, if you want separate hot/cold).

Running solo & self-supported

  • Drop bags are your main resupply tool without a crew — allowed at 7 of 12 checkpoints (Bullocks Flat I & II, Charlotte Pass Resort, Guthega Power Station I & II, Perisher Ski Tube, Village Green) plus the Finish. Worth planning contents deliberately for each.
  • The 4 designated pacer pick-up points (Guthega Power Stations, Perisher Ski Tube, Bullocks Flat II, Village Green) don't apply to you — solo through the entire back half, including the remote overnight stretch from Guthega to Ngarigo.
  • No crew to notice if something's wrong — the emergency/lost procedures below matter more, not less. Know them before race day.
  • All mandatory gear must be self-carried at all times regardless — this is the same for every runner, crewed or not.

Safety & emergency — quick reference

  • Event Safety & Medical Team: 0418 902 842 — call for any emergency, first aid, rescue, or if lost.
  • Emergency Services: 000 — if the Safety Team is unreachable.
  • Event Control Centre (withdrawal from anywhere other than a checkpoint): 0447 224 132.
  • Mobile coverage is patchy — in a dead zone, follow the course or head to higher ground; don't leave the track. Text may succeed where a call fails.
  • If lost: use the Avenza app + course map (download before race day), stop and think before backtracking, try phones from different providers, move toward higher ground for reception. If truly stuck: whistle 3 blasts repeated, shout "HELP" at intervals, lay bright gear out in the open.
  • Lightning: get to an enclosed building/vehicle; avoid peaks, ridges, tall trees and metal; if caught in the open, crouch with feet together, away from others.
  • Withdrawing: must be done at a checkpoint (sign the withdrawal form) unless medically evacuated — call the Event Control Centre immediately if you withdraw anywhere else, so they don't launch an unnecessary search.

Course & conditions

  • Alpine and highly changeable — from below 0°C to 25°C+, with rain, wind, snow or hail all possible within hours. Night minimum near 0°C even when Jindabyne feels mild.
  • Metal grated walkways on course — slow down or walk; easy to catch a toe, a fall here can be serious.
  • Hi-vis vest mandatory on Kosciuszko Road between Smiggins Hole and Perisher after 7pm, or whenever a marshal instructs.
  • Remote sections mean medical response can take 2+ hours — the mandatory gear list is the bare minimum for that reality, not a formality.
  • Marked with pink flagging tape (≥every 500m), reflective strips, red arrows/blue crosses at junctions, and distance signs every 5km. Personal GPS devices typically read 3–5% off the official distance.
  • Say no to NSAIDs (ibuprofen, Voltaren, Nurofen, naproxen) during the race — real risk combined with dehydration.
